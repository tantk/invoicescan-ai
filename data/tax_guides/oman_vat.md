# Oman — VAT Guide

## Overview
Oman introduced VAT on April 16, 2021, at a standard rate of 5%. VAT is administered by the Oman Tax Authority (OTA). Oman was the fourth GCC member state to implement VAT, following the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Bahrain. The VAT system is governed by the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2020) and the Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decision 53/2021).

## Tax Rates
- **Standard rate: 5%**
- **Zero-rated (0%)**: Exports of goods and services outside Oman, international transport of goods and passengers, supply of crude oil and natural gas, investment-grade precious metals (gold, silver, platinum 99%+ purity), basic food items (specific list), medicines and medical equipment (specific list), educational services and related goods
- **Exempt (no VAT, no input credit)**: Certain financial services (interest-based), local passenger transport, residential property rental, bare land, healthcare services provided by government or licensed institutions

## VAT Registration
- **Mandatory**: Taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 in the past 12 months (or expected in the next 12 months)
- **Voluntary**: Taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250 in the past 12 months (or expected in the next 12 months)
- **Non-resident**: Must register from the first taxable supply if not covered by reverse charge
- **Tax Identification Number (TIN)**: Issued by the OTA upon registration — also known as VATIN

## Invoice Requirements
### Tax Invoice (mandatory for all taxable supplies)
1. "Tax Invoice" label clearly displayed
2. Supplier name, address, and VATIN
3. Recipient name, address, and VATIN (if registered)
4. Unique sequential invoice number
5. Date of issue
6. Date of supply (if different from invoice date)
7. Description of goods or services supplied
8. Quantity or volume of supply
9. Unit price exclusive of VAT
10. Discount amount (if applicable)
11. VAT rate applied per line item
12. VAT amount payable per line item
13. Total amount exclusive of VAT
14. Total VAT amount
15. Total consideration inclusive of VAT
16. Currency (if not OMR, must state the exchange rate and OMR equivalent for VAT)

### Simplified Tax Invoice (permitted for B2C supplies up to OMR 500)
1. Supplier name and VATIN
2. Date of issue
3. Description of goods or services
4. Total amount inclusive of VAT
5. VAT amount

## Reverse Charge
Applies when:
- A registered person receives services from a non-resident supplier
- Importing goods where the importer is VAT-registered
- The recipient must self-account for VAT on their return

## E-Invoicing (Fawtara)
- **Pilot phase**: August 2026 for selected large taxpayers
- **Full rollout**: Expected to expand to all VAT-registered taxpayers by 2027–2028
- The Fawtara system will require real-time or near-real-time digital transmission of invoices to the OTA
- Businesses should begin preparing systems for integration

## Filing and Payment
- **Filing frequency**: Quarterly VAT returns (standard)
- **Due date**: Within 30 days after the end of each quarter
- **Payment**: Must accompany the return filing in OMR
- **Penalties**: Fines for late filing (OMR 25 per day, up to OMR 5,000); late payment incurs 1% per month on outstanding VAT

## Input Tax Recovery
- Full recovery on inputs related to taxable supplies (standard or zero-rated)
- No recovery on inputs related to exempt supplies
- Apportionment required for mixed-use inputs
- Blocked input tax: entertainment, motor vehicles (unless for business of resale/rental), club membership fees

## Common Pitfalls
1. Confusing zero-rated and exempt supplies — zero-rated allows input VAT recovery, exempt does not
2. Forgetting reverse charge on imported services
3. Not converting foreign currency invoices to OMR for VAT calculation
4. Missing the quarterly filing deadline — daily penalties accumulate quickly
5. Not registering when the threshold is met — retrospective registration may be required with back-dated VAT liability
6. Assuming all food is zero-rated — only items on the specific government-published list qualify
7. Not preparing for the Fawtara e-invoicing mandate starting August 2026
