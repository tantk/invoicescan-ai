# Norway — MVA (Merverdiavgift) Guide

## Overview
Norway's VAT, known as MVA (Merverdiavgift), is administered by the Skatteetaten (Norwegian Tax Administration). Norway is NOT an EU member state but is part of the European Economic Area (EEA). As such, Norway's VAT system is broadly aligned with EU VAT principles but has its own independent legislation (the Norwegian VAT Act / Merverdiavgiftsloven). Norway does not participate in the EU VAT Directive, VIES, or the One-Stop Shop (OSS) scheme.

## Tax Rates
- **Standard rate: 25%** (allmenn sats)
- **Reduced rate: 15%** (redusert sats) — foodstuffs and non-alcoholic beverages (not restaurant food)
- **Reduced rate: 12%** (lav sats) — passenger transport, hotel/accommodation, admission to cinemas, amusement parks, museums, sporting events, and other cultural activities; broadcasting services (NRK license fee abolished, but rate applies to streaming/media in certain contexts)
- **Zero-rated (0%)**: Exports of goods, certain international transport services, supply of vessels and aircraft in international trade, electric vehicles (newspapers were zero-rated, moved to exempt)
- **Exempt (no VAT, no input credit)**: Financial and insurance services, healthcare, education, residential property rental, cultural services by public institutions, certain artistic/literary services, electronic news services (newspapers/media from 2023)

## Tax ID Format
- **Organisasjonsnummer** (Organization number): 9 digits
  - Example: `123 456 789`
- **MVA-nummer** (VAT number): Organization number + "MVA" suffix
  - Format: `NO` + 9 digits + `MVA`
  - Example: `NO123456789MVA`
- Verify at: https://w2.brreg.no/enhet/sok/ (Bronnoysund Register Centre)

## Invoice Requirements
### Full VAT Invoice (salgsdokument)
1. Invoice date (fakturadato)
2. Unique sequential invoice number (fakturanummer) — must be generated through certified invoicing software or pre-printed forms
3. Seller's full name and address
4. Seller's organisasjonsnummer followed by "MVA" (e.g., 123456789MVA)
5. Buyer's full name and address
6. Buyer's organisasjonsnummer (for B2B transactions)
7. Date of supply (leveringsdato) if different from invoice date
8. Description of goods or services
9. Quantity and unit price (exclusive of MVA)
10. Any discount amount
11. Net amount per MVA rate
12. MVA rate(s) applied (25%, 15%, 12%)
13. MVA amount per rate in NOK
14. Total amount inclusive of MVA
15. For exempt supplies: clear statement of the exemption and legal basis
16. For reverse charge: "Omvendt avgiftsplikt" or "Reverse charge" notation
17. Payment terms and bank account details

### Simplified Invoice (forenklet salgsdokument, for supplies up to NOK 50,000 including MVA)
1. Seller's name and organisasjonsnummer with "MVA"
2. Invoice date
3. Description of goods or services
4. Total amount inclusive of MVA
5. MVA rate(s)

## Key Differences: Norway vs. EU
- **Not in EU VAT system**: No VIES validation, no intra-community supply rules, no EC Sales List
- **No OSS scheme**: Norway does not participate in the EU One-Stop Shop — businesses selling B2C into the EU must register for VAT in EU member states individually (or use the EU's Import One-Stop Shop for low-value goods)
- **VOEC scheme**: Norway has its own simplified registration scheme (VOEC — VAT on E-Commerce) for non-resident sellers of low-value goods (under NOK 3,000) and electronic/digital services to Norwegian consumers
- **Customs**: Imports from the EU are treated the same as imports from any other country — customs declarations and import VAT apply

## E-Invoicing
- **B2G**: Mandatory since 2012 — all invoices to Norwegian public sector must be electronic (EHF format — Elektronisk Handelsformat, based on Peppol BIS)
- **B2B**: Not yet mandatory — widely used but paper/PDF still valid
- **EHF format**: Norwegian national e-invoice standard, aligned with Peppol BIS Billing 3.0
- **Future plans**: Proposals for mandatory B2B e-invoicing from approximately 2028, aligned with broader Scandinavian and EEA digital reporting trends
- **Archiving**: Invoices must be stored for **5 years** (per the Bookkeeping Act / Bokforingsloven)

## Key Rules
- **Registration threshold**: NOK 50,000 taxable turnover in a 12-month period. No threshold for non-resident businesses.
- **VOEC registration**: Non-resident suppliers of electronic services, low-value goods, or digital services to Norwegian consumers must register via the VOEC scheme (no threshold for e-services)
- **Filing frequency**: Bi-monthly (every 2 months) for most businesses; annual for small businesses (turnover under NOK 1 million); monthly filing not standard but available
- **Tax point**: Generally when goods are delivered or services are performed
- **Reverse charge**: Applies to services purchased from non-resident suppliers; the Norwegian buyer self-accounts for MVA
- **Import VAT**: Declared on the VAT return (not paid at customs) for VAT-registered businesses since 2017

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Not EU**: Do not apply intra-community supply rules or use VIES for Norwegian VAT numbers — Norway is EEA, not EU
2. **Food rate confusion**: The 15% reduced rate applies to foodstuffs (groceries) but NOT to restaurant meals or takeaway food served for immediate consumption — restaurant food is 25%
3. **12% rate scope**: Cinema, passenger transport, hotels, and cultural events use 12% — but not all "entertainment" qualifies; check the specific list
4. **B2G invoicing**: Paper or PDF invoices to Norwegian public sector entities will be rejected — EHF electronic format is mandatory since 2012
5. **VOEC registration**: Foreign e-commerce and digital service sellers often miss the VOEC registration requirement for B2C sales to Norwegian consumers
6. **Import VAT on returns**: Since 2017, import VAT is declared on the VAT return, not at customs — businesses new to Norway often mishandle this
7. **Simplified invoice limit**: The NOK 50,000 threshold for simplified invoices is higher than many countries but still must not be used for B2B transactions where the buyer needs full input tax documentation
8. **Invoice numbering**: Numbers must be sequential and generated through certified software — manual or ad-hoc numbering systems are not compliant
