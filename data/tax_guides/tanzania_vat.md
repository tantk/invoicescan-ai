# Tanzania — VAT (Value Added Tax) Guide

## Overview

Tanzania's Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax on the supply of goods and services within the country, as well as imports. It is administered by the **TRA** (Tanzania Revenue Authority) under the Value Added Tax Act, 2014 (as amended by the Finance Act, 2025). The standard rate is 18%, with a new reduced rate of 16% for B2C electronic payment transactions introduced in September 2025. Compliance is enforced through mandatory Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFDs), and all taxpayers must issue fiscalized receipts/invoices for transactions. The Finance Act 2025 also introduced a VAT withholding regime.

## Tax Rates

- **Standard rate: 18%** — applies to most goods and services
- **Reduced rate: 16%** — applies to Business-to-Consumer (B2C) sales of goods where payment is made through electronic means (effective September 1, 2025)
- **Zero-rated (0%)**:
  - Exports of goods and services
  - Supplies to diplomats and international organizations
  - International transport services
  - Supplies to Special Economic Zones (SEZs)
  - Supply of certain agricultural inputs
- **Exempt**:
  - Unprocessed agricultural products
  - Medical and health services
  - Education services
  - Financial services (banking, insurance)
  - Public transport
  - Residential property leases
  - Supply of water by government bodies
  - Petroleum products (subject to separate fuel levy)
  - Agricultural equipment and inputs (tractors, ploughs, harvesters)
  - Live animals and unprocessed meat, fish, and dairy

## Tax ID Format

**TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)**

- **Format:**
  - Individuals: 10-digit number assigned by TRA
  - Businesses/Legal entities: 9-digit number assigned by TRA
- **Usage:** The TIN also serves as the VAT registration number for VAT-registered businesses.
- **Requirement:** All persons and entities carrying on taxable economic activity must register with TRA and obtain a TIN. VAT registration is mandatory for businesses with annual turnover of TZS 200 million (approx. USD 77,000) or more.
- **Example:** `100-123-456` (individual) or `12-345-678-9` (business)
- **Verification:** [TRA TIN Verification Portal](https://www.tra.go.tz/)

## Invoice Requirements

All VAT-registered businesses must issue fiscalized invoices/receipts through an approved EFD. Required fields include:

1. Supplier's business name (registered name)
2. Supplier's TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)
3. Supplier's VAT Registration Number
4. Supplier's physical address
5. Buyer's name (for B2B transactions)
6. Buyer's TIN and VAT Registration Number (for B2B transactions)
7. Unique EFD receipt/invoice number
8. Date and time of issue
9. Description of goods or services
10. Quantity and unit of measure
11. Unit price (exclusive of VAT)
12. Taxable amount
13. VAT rate applied (18% or 16%)
14. VAT amount
15. Total amount (inclusive of VAT)
16. EFD serial number
17. Fiscal receipt verification code
18. QR code (generated by EFD for real-time verification by TRA)

## E-Invoicing

- **Status:** Mandatory. All VAT-registered businesses must use approved Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFDs) to issue receipts and invoices.
- **EFD system (EFDMS):** The Electronic Fiscal Device Management System is TRA's centralized platform. All EFDs must be connected to TRA's system and transmit transaction data in real time.
- **Device types:**
  - EFD Receipting (EFD-R): For retail and B2C transactions
  - EFD Invoicing (EFD-I): For B2B transactions and larger businesses
- **Real-time transmission:** EFDs send invoice and receipt data in real time to TRA, which validates the information and returns a QR code that is printed on the receipt/invoice.
- **Protocol 2.1:** TRA requires all EFDs to run updated protocol 2.1 software for enhanced connectivity and data transmission.
- **System integration:** The Finance Act 2025 mandates that taxpayers' own invoicing/ERP systems must be interfaced/linked with TRA's EFD system.
- **Turnover threshold:** Businesses with annual turnover of TZS 14 million or more must issue fiscalized receipts.
- **Monthly filing:** VAT returns are filed monthly by the 20th of the following month through the TRA online portal.

## Key Rules

- **Mandatory EFD compliance:** TRA may disallow input VAT claims if not supported by proper fiscal receipts/invoices from an approved EFD. Penalties of up to TZS 4 million apply for not issuing EFD receipts.
- **VAT withholding regime (effective July 2025):** Designated agents (government institutions, VAT-registered persons appointed by the Commissioner General) must withhold:
  - 3% of the VAT amount for goods
  - 6% of the VAT amount for services
  - Withheld VAT must be remitted to TRA by the 20th of the following month
  - Suppliers receive a VAT Withholding Certificate to claim the withheld amount as input tax
- **B2C electronic payment discount:** The 16% reduced rate incentivizes electronic payments for B2C sales. Businesses must configure their EFDs to apply the correct rate based on payment method.
- **VAT deferment ending:** VAT deferment on imported capital goods will cease from June 30, 2026. Businesses relying on this deferment must plan for upfront VAT payments.
- **System integration deadline:** Businesses must ensure their invoicing systems are interfaced with TRA's EFD system per the Finance Act 2025 mandate.
- **Input tax claims:** Only fiscalized invoices/receipts support input VAT claims. Non-fiscalized documents are not valid for VAT deductions.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Not using an approved EFD** — Operating without an EFD (or with an outdated device) means all your invoices are non-fiscalized, and your buyers cannot claim input VAT. Penalties up to TZS 4 million apply.
2. **Failing to update EFD to protocol 2.1 software** — TRA requires the latest protocol. Older EFD software versions may not connect to TRA's system, rendering receipts invalid.
3. **Applying 16% rate without verifying electronic payment** — The reduced B2C rate only applies when payment is made electronically. Applying it to cash transactions is an undercharge of VAT.
4. **Not issuing VAT withholding certificates** — Designated withholding agents who withhold VAT from suppliers must issue certificates. Without the certificate, the supplier cannot claim the withheld VAT as input credit.
5. **Missing the 20th-of-month deadline for returns and withholding remittances** — Both VAT returns and withheld VAT must be remitted by the 20th. Late submission triggers penalties and interest.
6. **Not planning for the end of VAT deferment on capital goods** — After June 30, 2026, VAT on imported capital goods must be paid upfront. Businesses should budget accordingly.
7. **Failing to interface business systems with TRA's EFD system** — The Finance Act 2025 mandates system integration. Non-compliance creates a disconnect between reported and actual sales data.
8. **Accepting non-fiscalized invoices from suppliers** — Input VAT can only be claimed with fiscalized documents. Always verify the QR code and fiscal receipt number on supplier invoices.
