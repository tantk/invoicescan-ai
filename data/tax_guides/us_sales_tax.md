# United States — Sales Tax Guide

## Overview
The US has no federal sales tax. Sales tax is levied at state, county, city, and special district levels. Rates vary from 0% (Oregon, Montana, Delaware, New Hampshire, Alaska) to combined rates exceeding 10%.

## Tax Structure
- **State tax**: 0–7.25% (California highest at 7.25%)
- **County tax**: 0–5.5%
- **City tax**: 0–5%
- **Special district tax**: 0–3%
- Combined rates can exceed 10% in some jurisdictions

## Key State Rates (2026)
- California: 7.25% (up to 10.75% with local)
- Texas: 6.25% (up to 8.25% with local)
- New York: 4% (up to 8.875% in NYC)
- Florida: 6% (up to 8.5% with local)
- Illinois: 6.25% (up to 11% in Chicago)
- Washington: 6.5% (up to 10.5% with local)
- Tennessee: 7% (up to 9.75% with local)
- No sales tax: Oregon, Montana, Delaware, New Hampshire

## Exemptions
- Most states exempt groceries (food for home consumption)
- Prescription medicine is generally exempt
- Manufacturing equipment exempt in many states
- Resale purchases with valid resale certificate
- Nonprofit and government purchases (with documentation)

## Invoice Requirements
- Seller name and address
- Buyer name (for B2B)
- Invoice number and date
- Description of goods/services
- Tax amount shown separately (or "tax included" notation)
- EIN/FEIN for tax identification
- Resale certificate number if applicable

## Use Tax
When sales tax isn't collected (e.g., out-of-state purchases), buyers owe use tax at their local rate. Post-Wayfair (2018), most states require remote sellers to collect if they exceed economic nexus thresholds ($100K revenue or 200 transactions).

## Common Pitfalls
- SaaS/digital goods taxability varies wildly by state
- Shipping charges: taxable in some states, exempt in others
- Bundled transactions may be fully taxable even if some items are exempt
- Drop shipping creates complex nexus issues
