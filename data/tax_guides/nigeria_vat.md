# Nigeria — VAT Guide

## Overview
Nigeria's VAT is administered by the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS). Relatively low rate compared to global standards.

## Tax Rate
- **Standard rate: 7.5%** (increased from 5% on February 1, 2020)
- **Zero-rated (0%)**: Exports, goods purchased by diplomats
- **Exempt**: Basic food items, medical/pharmaceutical products, baby products, educational materials, agricultural equipment, plants and machinery for use in EPZs, all exports

## VAT Registration
- All businesses (no threshold — ALL taxable persons must register)
- TIN (Tax Identification Number): issued by Joint Tax Board
- VAT registration number: separate from TIN

## Invoice Requirements
1. Supplier name, address, TIN
2. Customer name, address, TIN
3. Invoice number and date
4. Description of goods/services
5. Quantity and value
6. VAT amount (7.5% shown separately)
7. Total amount

## Withholding VAT
- Since January 2024: large companies and government entities designated as VAT withholding agents
- Withhold VAT at point of payment and remit directly to FIRS
- Applies to contracts >NGN 50 million

## Filing
- Monthly VAT returns (due 21st of following month)
- Electronic filing via FIRS TaxPro Max platform
- Both VAT collected (output) and VAT paid (input) reported

## E-Invoicing
- No mandatory e-invoicing yet
- FIRS exploring electronic invoicing framework
- TaxPro Max platform handles electronic filing

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming there's a registration threshold (there isn't — all businesses must register)
- Not charging VAT on services (services are taxable in Nigeria)
- Exempt vs zero-rated confusion (exempt = no credit; zero-rated = credit allowed)
- Cross-border services: reverse charge applies to imported services
- Withholding agents not remitting withheld VAT
