# Japan — Consumption Tax Guide

## Overview
Japan's consumption tax (消費税, shōhizei) is a national VAT-type tax. Since October 2023, the Qualified Invoice System (QIS/インボイス制度) requires specific invoice formats for input tax credits.

## Tax Rates
- **Standard rate: 10%** (most goods and services)
- **Reduced rate: 8%** (food and non-alcoholic beverages for takeout/delivery, newspaper subscriptions 2+ per week)
- Dining in at restaurants: 10% (standard)
- Takeout food: 8% (reduced)
- Alcohol: 10% (standard)

## Qualified Invoice System (QIS)
Since October 1, 2023:
- Sellers must register to issue qualified invoices
- Registration issues a **QIIN** (Qualified Invoice Issuer Number): T + 13 digits
- Without a qualified invoice, buyers cannot claim full input tax credits
- Transitional measures: 80% credit allowed through Sept 2026, 50% through Sept 2029

## QIIN Format
`T1234567890123`
- Prefix: T
- 13 digits (matches corporate number for companies, unique for individuals)
- Verify at: https://www.invoice-kohyo.nta.go.jp

## Invoice Requirements (Qualified Invoice)
1. Seller name and QIIN
2. Transaction date
3. Description of goods/services
4. Price per item (tax-exclusive or tax-inclusive, clearly marked)
5. **Applicable tax rate per item** (8% or 10%)
6. **Tax amount per rate category** (must show separately)
7. Total amount
8. Buyer name (B2B)

## Simplified Qualified Invoice
For retail, food service, taxi, ≤¥30,000 transactions:
- QIIN required
- Buyer name NOT required
- Tax rate can be shown as combined

## Key Differences from Standard Invoice
- Must clearly separate 8% and 10% rated items
- Tax amount must be calculated per rate, then summed (not calculated on grand total)
- Rounding: fraction per rate category (cut, round, or round up — chosen by seller consistently)

## E-Invoicing
- Japan joined Peppol in 2021
- Digital invoices accepted (PDF, electronic data)
- No mandatory e-invoicing format yet, but Peppol adoption growing
- Digital preservation law requires proper electronic storage

## Common Pitfalls
- Mixing 8% and 10% items without showing breakdown per rate
- Calculating tax on the combined total instead of per-rate subtotals
- Non-registered sellers issuing invoices (buyers lose input credit)
- Incorrect treatment of takeout vs dine-in (8% vs 10%)
- Not showing QIIN on invoice (invalid for credit claims)
