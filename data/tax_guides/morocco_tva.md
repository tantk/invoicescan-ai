# Morocco — TVA (Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée) Guide

## Overview

Morocco's Value Added Tax, known as TVA (Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée), is a consumption tax levied on the sale of goods, provision of services, and imports. It is administered by the **DGI** (Direction Générale des Impôts), the General Directorate of Taxes. Morocco has been undergoing a multi-year rate convergence plan (2024-2026) to simplify rates down to two main rates: 10% and 20%. The country began mandatory e-invoicing in early 2026 through the Simpl-TVA platform, after a pilot phase in late 2025.

## Tax Rates

As of 2026, following the completion of the rate convergence plan:

- **Standard rate: 20%** — applies to most goods and services
- **Reduced rate: 10%** — applies to:
  - Restaurant and hotel accommodation services
  - Certain food products (edible oils, pasta, salt, canned sardines)
  - Electricity (including from renewable energy sources)
  - Urban and road transport operations
  - Banking and credit operations
  - Certain professional services (lawyers, notaries, architects, etc.)
  - Rental of industrial/commercial equipment
  - Refined sugar
  - Cinematographic works
- **Exempt with credit (equivalent to zero-rated)**:
  - Exports of goods and services
  - Goods and services delivered to export enterprises in free zones
  - International transport
  - Agricultural equipment (for certain categories)
  - Investment goods for the first 36 months of a new business
- **Exempt without credit**:
  - Basic food products (bread, couscous, flour, cereals, milk, fresh meat — including seasoned meat per 2025 Finance Act)
  - Pharmaceutical products
  - Educational services
  - Medical services and hospitalization
  - Certain financial transactions
  - Property transactions (certain conditions)
  - Newspapers and publications
  - Artisanal products sold locally

**Note:** The old 7% and 14% rates have been phased out under the 2024-2026 convergence plan, with items transitioning to either 10% or 20%.

## Tax ID Format

**ICE (Identifiant Commun des Entreprises) & IF (Identifiant Fiscal)**

- **ICE:**
  - **Format:** 15 digits
  - **Structure:** First 9 digits = legal unit identification; next 4 digits = establishment identifier (0000 for single-establishment businesses); last 2 digits = control key
  - **Mandatory on all invoices** under Morocco's commercial code
  - **Example:** `001234567000012`

- **IF (Identifiant Fiscal):**
  - **Format:** 8 digits
  - **Assigned by:** DGI upon tax registration
  - **Usage:** All tax-related matters (IS, IR, TVA declarations), DGI correspondence, Simpl portal access
  - **VAT designation:** TVA-registered entities have a "TVA" suffix added to their IF
  - **Example:** `12345678`

- **Verification:** [DGI Simpl Portal](https://www.tax.gov.ma/)

## Invoice Requirements

1. Invoice title ("Facture" clearly indicated)
2. Sequential invoice number
3. Date of issue
4. Supplier's full legal name
5. Supplier's ICE (15-digit identifier)
6. Supplier's IF (Identifiant Fiscal)
7. Supplier's TVA registration number (IF + TVA suffix)
8. Supplier's address and registered office
9. Supplier's trade register (RC) number
10. Buyer's full legal name
11. Buyer's ICE
12. Buyer's IF
13. Buyer's address
14. Description of goods or services
15. Quantity and unit of measure
16. Unit price (excluding TVA)
17. Discount amounts (if applicable)
18. Net taxable amount per TVA rate
19. TVA rate(s) applied (10% or 20%)
20. TVA amount
21. Total amount inclusive of TVA
22. Payment terms and due date
23. Currency (if not MAD, applicable exchange rate)

## E-Invoicing

- **Status:** Mandatory for all eligible businesses from early 2026, following a pilot phase that began in October 2025.
- **Platform:** Simpl-TVA, managed by DGI, is Morocco's official digital system for issuing, validating, and managing e-invoices.
- **Format:** Universal Business Language (UBL) and Cross-Industry Invoice (CII) formats — internationally standardized formats.
- **Model:** Post-audit (decentralized) — businesses can freely exchange invoices, with DGI conducting checks after submission. Similar to the 4-corner model used in Belgium.
- **Timeline:**
  - October 2024: E-invoicing proposals and public consultations
  - October 2025: Pilot phase began
  - Early 2026: Full mandatory implementation
- **Registration:** Companies must register on the Simpl-TVA platform to issue compliant invoices, submit them for validation, and maintain full traceability.
- **Monthly filing:** TVA returns are filed monthly (for annual turnover above MAD 1 million) or quarterly (for turnover below MAD 1 million) through the Simpl portal.

## Key Rules

- **Rate convergence completed:** By 2026, Morocco has converged to two main rates (10% and 20%) plus exemptions. Businesses must update systems to reflect the elimination of the old 7% and 14% rates.
- **ICE on all documents:** Both supplier and buyer ICE must appear on sales invoices, credit notes, debit notes, and quotations. Failure to include ICE may result in rejection of input tax credit claims.
- **Distinction between exempt-with-credit and exempt-without-credit:** Exempt-with-credit (exports, free zones) allows recovery of input TVA. Exempt-without-credit (basic food, medical) does not. This distinction is critical for input tax recovery.
- **Prorata deduction:** Businesses making both taxable and exempt supplies must calculate a prorata coefficient to determine the recoverable portion of input TVA.
- **Investment goods TVA recovery:** New businesses can recover TVA on investment goods purchased in the first 36 months, subject to proper documentation.
- **Reverse charge on imported services:** Registered persons receiving services from non-resident providers must self-assess and pay TVA.
- **Statute of limitations:** DGI can audit and reassess TVA for the current year plus the four preceding fiscal years.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Still using old 7% or 14% rates** — These have been phased out under the 2024-2026 convergence. All items previously at 7% or 14% have transitioned to 10% or 20%. Update rate tables immediately.
2. **Omitting ICE on invoices** — The ICE is mandatory for both supplier and buyer. Invoices missing the ICE are non-compliant and may result in denied input tax deductions.
3. **Confusing exempt-with-credit and exempt-without-credit** — Treating an exempt-without-credit supply as zero-rated (exempt with credit) leads to improper input TVA recovery and penalties upon audit.
4. **Not registering on Simpl-TVA** — Since early 2026, e-invoicing through the Simpl-TVA platform is mandatory. Operating outside the system is non-compliant.
5. **Incorrect prorata calculation** — Businesses with mixed supplies must accurately calculate their recovery coefficient. Errors lead to over- or under-recovery of input TVA.
6. **Missing the monthly/quarterly filing deadline** — Late filings attract penalties of 15% surcharge for the first month plus 0.5% per additional month, in addition to interest at 6% per annum.
7. **Failing to apply reverse charge on imported services** — Services from foreign providers are taxable in Morocco. Not self-assessing TVA creates an underpayment that attracts penalties upon audit.
8. **Not maintaining invoices for the audit period** — DGI can audit up to 4 years back. All invoices and supporting documents must be retained for at least 5 years.
