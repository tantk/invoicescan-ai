# Bangladesh — VAT (Value Added Tax) Guide

## Overview

Bangladesh's Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax levied on the supply of goods and services within the country. It is administered by the **NBR** (National Board of Revenue) under the Value Added Tax and Supplementary Duty Act, 2012. Bangladesh operates a multi-rate VAT system with a standard rate of 15% and several reduced/truncated rates for specific sectors. Compliance is enforced through mandatory Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFD) and Sales Data Controllers (SDC) for qualifying businesses. The system is governed by regulatory instruments including SRO No. 182-Law/2025/310-VAT (effective FY 2025-2026).

## Tax Rates

- **Standard rate: 15%** — applies to most goods and services where annual turnover exceeds BDT 8 million
- **Truncated/Reduced rates:**
  - **5%** — Trading activities (buying and selling goods without manufacturing)
  - **2.4%** — Supply of medicine and pharmaceutical products
  - **2%** — Diesel, kerosene, octane, petrol, furnace oil, and LP gas
  - **7.5%** — Certain specified services and goods
  - **10%** — Certain services including certain professional services
- **Zero-rated (0%)**:
  - Exports of goods
  - Supplies to Export Processing Zones (EPZs)
  - Jute and jute goods for export
  - Deemed exports
- **Exempt**:
  - Unprocessed agricultural products
  - Fresh fish, meat, and poultry
  - Rice, wheat, and flour
  - Vegetables and fruits (unprocessed)
  - Children's food (baby formula)
  - Books, newspapers, and periodicals
  - Education services
  - Health and medical services
  - Public transport
  - Agricultural equipment and inputs
  - Certain government services

## Tax ID Format

**BIN (Business Identification Number)**

- **Format:** 9-digit unique identifier
- **Issued by:** National Board of Revenue (NBR) upon VAT registration
- **Requirement:** Every business with annual turnover exceeding BDT 8 million (approx. USD 73,000) must obtain a BIN
- **Example:** `123456789`
- **Registration:** Online through the NBR's Integrated VAT Administration System (IVAS) at [vat.gov.bd](https://vat.gov.bd)
- **Note:** The BIN is used for all VAT-related filings, communications, and displayed on all business documents

## Invoice Requirements

Invoices must use the prescribed **Mushak 6.3** form and include:

1. Form title "Mushak 6.3" clearly printed
2. Supplier's name and business name
3. Supplier's BIN (Business Identification Number)
4. Supplier's address (registered and trading)
5. Buyer's name
6. Buyer's BIN (mandatory for B2B transactions)
7. Buyer's address
8. Invoice serial number (unique and sequential)
9. Date of issue
10. Description of goods or services
11. Quantity and unit of measure
12. Unit price (excluding VAT)
13. Total value (excluding VAT)
14. VAT rate applicable
15. VAT amount
16. Supplementary Duty amount (if applicable)
17. Total amount payable (including VAT and SD)
18. EFD/SDC fiscal receipt number (if issued through a fiscal device)
19. Signature of the authorized person

## E-Invoicing

- **Status:** Mandatory for designated businesses through Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFD) and Sales Data Controllers (SDC).
- **EFD (Electronic Fiscal Device):** Hardware-based fiscal devices that record and transmit transaction data to NBR in real time. Required for retail businesses and service providers above a certain turnover threshold.
- **SDC (Sales Data Controller):** Software-based controllers installed in business premises that integrate with existing POS systems to capture and transmit sales data to NBR.
- **PKI (Public Key Infrastructure):** Digital signature system used for secure e-filing and document authentication.
- **Platform:** NBR's Integrated VAT Administration System (IVAS) for online return filing and compliance.
- **VDS exemption:** VAT Deduction at Source (VDS) is not required if the VAT invoice (fiscal receipt) is issued using EFD, SDC, PKI, or POS system, provided the buyer's name and BIN are on the receipt.
- **Monthly return:** Filed electronically via Mushak 9.1 form through the NBR online portal by the 15th of the following month.

## Key Rules

- **Mandatory BIN for threshold businesses:** Any business with annual turnover exceeding BDT 8 million must register for VAT and obtain a BIN.
- **VAT Deducted at Source (VDS):** Certain designated entities (government bodies, large corporations, NGOs) must withhold VAT at source when making payments to suppliers. VDS rates vary by type of supply (as defined in SRO No. 182-Law/2025/310-VAT).
- **Mushak forms are prescriptive:** Bangladesh uses specific numbered forms for invoices (6.3), credit notes (6.6), debit notes (6.7), and returns (9.1). Using non-prescribed formats results in non-compliance.
- **Supplementary Duty (SD):** Charged in addition to VAT on certain luxury and sin goods (tobacco, alcohol, soft drinks, vehicles). SD is calculated before VAT — VAT is then charged on the SD-inclusive price.
- **Input tax credit:** Available only if the purchase invoice contains the supplier's BIN and all mandatory fields. Invoices from unregistered suppliers do not qualify.
- **Advance Trade VAT (ATV):** Importers pay VAT at the import stage, which can be adjusted as input tax credit against domestic sales.
- **Penalty for non-issuance:** Failure to issue a proper Mushak 6.3 invoice triggers a penalty of BDT 10,000 per instance or 2% of the value, whichever is higher.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Not obtaining a BIN when turnover exceeds BDT 8 million** — Operating without VAT registration above the threshold is a criminal offense under the VAT Act, with penalties including fines and imprisonment.
2. **Using non-prescribed invoice formats** — The Mushak 6.3 format is mandatory. Invoices in any other format are not valid for VAT purposes and cannot be used for input tax credits.
3. **Ignoring VDS obligations** — Designated withholding entities must deduct VAT at source per SRO rates. Failure to withhold makes the withholding entity liable for the un-withheld amount plus penalty.
4. **Not installing required EFD/SDC devices** — Businesses notified by NBR to install fiscal devices must comply. Non-compliance results in penalties and potential business closure orders.
5. **Missing the monthly Mushak 9.1 filing deadline** — Returns must be filed by the 15th of the following month. Late filing attracts a penalty of BDT 10,000 per month of delay.
6. **Failing to display BIN at business premises** — The BIN certificate must be prominently displayed. NBR inspectors can issue on-the-spot fines for non-display.
7. **Not applying Supplementary Duty before VAT** — SD must be calculated first and added to the base price before applying VAT. Calculating them in the wrong order results in incorrect tax amounts.
8. **Claiming input credit on exempt supplies** — Input VAT on purchases related to exempt supplies cannot be claimed as credit. Businesses making both taxable and exempt supplies must apportion input credits.
