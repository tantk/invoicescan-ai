# India — State GST (SGST) Supplementary Guide

## Overview
This guide supplements the base India GST guide with detailed state-specific rules. While GST rates are uniform across India (set by the GST Council), states have significant variations in registration requirements, e-way bill thresholds, professional tax levies, and administrative procedures. Understanding these state-level differences is critical for businesses operating across multiple Indian states.

## GST Structure Recap
- **Intra-state transactions**: CGST (Central) + SGST (State) — each at half the total GST rate
  - Example: 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST
- **Inter-state transactions**: IGST (Integrated) at the full rate
  - Example: 18% IGST
- **Union Territories without legislature**: CGST + UTGST (instead of SGST)
  - Applies to: Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Ladakh

## GST Rate Slabs (2026 — Post GST 2.0 Reforms)
Following the 56th GST Council meeting and GST 2.0 reforms (effective September 22, 2025):
- **0%**: Essential items (unprocessed food grains, fresh fruits and vegetables, milk, healthcare, education)
- **5%**: Everyday necessities (packaged food, economy transport, small restaurant meals, basic clothing under INR 1,000)
- **18%**: Standard rate for most goods and services (IT services, telecom, financial services, electronics, processed foods, restaurants in hotels)
- **40%**: Luxury and sin goods (previously 28% + cess items — luxury cars, tobacco, aerated drinks, pan masala)
- **Compensation Cess**: Still applicable on certain luxury items on top of the applicable rate

## GSTIN Format and State Codes
The GSTIN (GST Identification Number) is a 15-digit alphanumeric code:
```
22AAAAA0000A1Z5
│  │          │ │ └── Check digit
│  │          │ └──── Default 'Z'
│  │          └────── Entity number within same state (1-9, A-Z)
│  └───────────────── PAN (10 characters)
└──────────────────── State code (2 digits)
```

**2026 Updates**:
- E-Way Bill 2.0 portal launched alongside existing portal — data syncs automatically

## State-Specific Professional Tax Rates
Professional tax is a **state-level levy** separate from GST. It applies to salaried employees and professionals. Maximum constitutional limit is INR 2,500 per year. Rates vary significantly:

|-------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Maharashtra** | INR 200/month (salary INR 10,001+); INR 300/month (salary INR 15,001+) in February | INR 2,500 |
| **Karnataka** | INR 200/month (salary INR 25,001+) | INR 2,400 |
| **West Bengal** | INR 150/month (salary INR 25,001-40,000); INR 200/month (salary INR 40,001+) | INR 2,400 |
| **Tamil Nadu** | Half-yearly payment based on salary slabs | INR 2,500 |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | INR 200/month (salary INR 20,001+) | INR 2,500 |
| **Telangana** | INR 200/month (salary INR 20,001+) | INR 2,500 |
| **Gujarat** | INR 200/month (salary INR 12,001+) | INR 2,400 |
| **Madhya Pradesh** | INR 208/month (salary INR 25,001+) | INR 2,500 |

**States with no professional tax**: Rajasthan, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Delhi (UT), and several northeastern states.

## SGST vs. UTGST
| Feature | SGST | UTGST |
|---------|------|-------|
| **Full Name** | State Goods and Services Tax | Union Territory Goods and Services Tax |
| **Administered by** | State Government | Central Government (on behalf of UT) |
| **Applicable in** | All states with legislature | UTs without legislature (Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar, Ladakh) |
| **Revenue** | Goes to the respective state | Goes to the respective UT treasury |
| **Rate** | Equal to CGST (half of total GST) | Equal to CGST (half of total GST) |
| **Law** | Individual state SGST Acts | UTGST Act, 2017 (central legislation) |
| **Input Tax Credit** | SGST credit can offset SGST liability in same state; excess can offset IGST | UTGST credit can offset UTGST liability; excess can offset IGST |

**Important ITC Rule**: SGST credit from one state **cannot** be used to offset SGST liability in another state. Each state's SGST operates independently. For cross-state credit utilization, the order is: IGST first, then CGST, then SGST/UTGST.

## State-Specific GST Registration Requirements
While the basic registration threshold is INR 40 lakh (goods) and INR 20 lakh (services) in most states, **special category states** have lower thresholds:

### Special Category States (INR 20 lakh for goods, INR 10 lakh for services):
- Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura, Uttarakhand

### Standard States (INR 40 lakh for goods, INR 20 lakh for services):
- All other states and UTs

### Multiple Registrations
- A business operating in multiple states must obtain **separate GSTIN** for each state
- Each state registration files its own returns and maintains independent books
- Input tax credit is state-specific — SGST paid in Maharashtra cannot be credited against SGST payable in Karnataka

## Key State-Level Compliance Differences

### E-Invoicing
- Mandatory for all businesses with aggregate annual turnover (AATO) exceeding **INR 5 crore**
- Uniform across all states — generated through IRP (Invoice Registration Portal)
- Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and QR code required on all e-invoices

### GST Returns Filing
- All states follow the same return filing schedule (GSTR-1 by 11th/13th, GSTR-3B by 20th of following month)
- However, state-level authorities conduct **separate audits and assessments** for SGST components
- Annual return (GSTR-9) due by December 31 for the previous financial year

### Advance Ruling
- Each state has its own Authority for Advance Ruling (AAR)
- Rulings from one state AAR are **not binding** on other states
- This can lead to conflicting interpretations — e.g., different states may classify the same product under different HSN codes

## Common Pitfalls (State-Specific)
1. **Wrong state code in GSTIN**: Using the incorrect state code results in SGST going to the wrong state and denial of ITC for the buyer
2. **Inter-state vs. intra-state confusion**: Bill-to and ship-to addresses in different states trigger IGST, not CGST+SGST — even if the supplier is in the same state as one of the addresses
3. **E-way bill state threshold variations**: Generating e-way bills based on the national INR 50,000 threshold when the state has a higher threshold (INR 1,00,000 in Maharashtra) — or worse, not generating one when the state has a lower or different threshold
4. **Professional tax non-compliance**: Operating in a state that levies professional tax without registering and deducting it from employee salaries
5. **Multiple state registrations**: Failing to register in each state where the business has a place of business or makes taxable supplies — each state requires a separate GSTIN
6. **Conflicting AAR rulings**: Relying on an advance ruling from one state when operating in another — AAR rulings are jurisdiction-specific
7. **SGST credit cannot cross state lines**: Attempting to use SGST paid in one state against SGST liability in another state — this is not permitted under GST law
8. **Special category state thresholds**: Applying the standard INR 40 lakh registration threshold in special category states where the threshold is INR 20 lakh
