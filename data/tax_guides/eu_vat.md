# European Union — VAT Guide

## Overview
VAT (Value Added Tax) is applied at each stage of the supply chain. The EU sets minimum rates but each member state sets its own standard and reduced rates.

## Standard Rates by Country (2026)
- Hungary: 27% (highest in EU)
- Denmark, Sweden, Croatia: 25%
- Finland, Greece, Ireland, Poland, Portugal: 23-24%
- Belgium, Czech Republic, Latvia, Lithuania, Netherlands, Spain, Austria, Italy: 21-22%
- France, UK (pre-Brexit): 20%
- Germany: 19%
- Luxembourg: 17% (lowest in EU)

## Reduced Rates
Most EU countries have 1-2 reduced rates (typically 5-13%) for:
- Food and drink
- Books and newspapers
- Pharmaceuticals
- Passenger transport
- Hotels and accommodation
- Cultural events

## Reverse Charge Mechanism
When a business buys from another EU country:
- Supplier charges 0% VAT
- Buyer self-assesses and pays VAT in their own country
- Invoice must state: "Reverse charge — Article 194/196 VAT Directive"
- Both parties' VAT numbers required

## Intra-Community Supply
Sale of goods between EU businesses:
- 0% VAT (exempt with right to deduct)
- Buyer must have valid VAT number (check via VIES)
- Invoice must reference: "Exempt intra-Community supply — Article 138(1)"
- Proof of transport required

## Invoice Requirements (VAT Directive 2006/112/EC)
1. Sequential invoice number
2. Date of issue
3. Date of supply (if different)
4. Supplier name, address, VAT number
5. Customer name, address, VAT number (B2B)
6. Description of goods/services
7. Quantity
8. Unit price (excluding VAT)
9. VAT rate applied
10. VAT amount
11. Total amount (including VAT)
12. Currency
13. Reference to exemption/reverse charge if applicable
14. "Self-billing" if applicable

## E-Invoicing (EN 16931)
- B2G mandatory in most member states
- B2B mandates rolling out: Italy (2019), France (2026), Germany (2027), Spain (2025)
- Peppol network for cross-border
- Formats: UBL 2.1 or CII (EN 16931 compliant)

## VAT Groups & Special Schemes
- Small business exemption thresholds vary by country
- Margin scheme for second-hand goods
- Travel agents' special scheme
- Cash accounting scheme
- Flat-rate scheme for farmers

## Common Pitfalls
- Charging domestic VAT on B2B cross-border sales (should be reverse charge)
- Missing VAT number validation via VIES
- Incorrect triangulation in chain transactions
- Not adjusting for customer's country rate in B2C digital services (OSS scheme)
- Place of supply rules for services differ from goods
