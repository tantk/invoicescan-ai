# Australia — GST Guide

## Overview
Australia's Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a broad-based 10% tax on most goods, services, and imports. Administered by the Australian Taxation Office (ATO).

## Tax Rate
- **Standard rate: 10%**
- **GST-free (0%)**: Basic food, health, education, childcare, exports, some financial services
- **Input-taxed (no GST, no credits)**: Residential rent, financial supplies, precious metals

## ABN (Australian Business Number)
- 11 digits: `XX XXX XXX XXX`
- Mandatory on all tax invoices
- Verify at: https://abr.business.gov.au
- Without ABN, payer must withhold 47% from payment

## Tax Invoice Requirements
For sales >A$82.50 (including GST):
1. Document must be labeled "Tax Invoice"
2. Seller's identity (name or trading name)
3. Seller's ABN
4. Invoice date
5. Description of items (brief)
6. For each item: quantity and price
7. GST amount for each item OR statement "Total price includes GST"
8. Total price

### Additional requirements for invoices >A$1,000:
9. Buyer's identity (name or ABN)
10. Buyer's address

## GST Calculation
- GST = Price × 1/11 (when price includes GST)
- GST = Price × 10% (when price excludes GST)
- Total = Price + GST

## BAS (Business Activity Statement)
- Quarterly or monthly reporting
- Reports: GST collected, GST paid (input credits), net GST payable/refundable
- Due 28 days after quarter end

## Common Pitfalls
- Forgetting "Tax Invoice" label (technically required)
- Missing ABN triggers 47% withholding
- Claiming GST credits on input-taxed supplies
- Not applying GST to digital imports (Netflix tax since 2017)
- Wine Equalisation Tax (WET) is separate from GST — 29% on wholesale wine value
