# Philippines — VAT Guide

## Overview
Philippines VAT is administered by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). The system covers most goods and services with a single standard rate.

## Tax Rate
- **Standard rate: 12%**
- **Zero-rated (0%)**: Exports, foreign currency-denominated sales (BOI-registered), services to non-residents, power generation from renewable energy
- **VAT-exempt**: Agricultural food products (original state), educational services, healthcare, residential rent ≤PHP 15,000/month, sale of residential lot ≤PHP 1.5M or house ≤PHP 2.5M, cooperatives, senior citizen/PWD discounts

## VAT Registration
- **Mandatory**: Gross annual sales/receipts >PHP 3 million
- **Percentage tax**: Businesses ≤PHP 3M pay 3% Percentage Tax instead of VAT
- TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number): 9 digits + branch code: `XXX-XXX-XXX-XXX`

## Official Receipt vs Sales Invoice
- **Sales Invoice (SI)**: For sale of goods
- **Official Receipt (OR)**: For sale of services or payment acknowledgment
- Both must be BIR-registered (Authority to Print or CAS permit)
- Sequential numbering required
- Must show TIN prominently

## Invoice Requirements
### VAT Sales Invoice / Official Receipt
1. Seller name, TIN, address
2. Buyer name, TIN, address (B2B)
3. Date
4. Sequential invoice/receipt number
5. Description of goods/services
6. Quantity and unit price
7. Amount of sale (VAT-exclusive)
8. VAT amount (12%)
9. Total amount
10. BIR permit number (Authority to Print or CAS)
11. For receipts >PHP 100: zero-rated or VAT-exempt notation if applicable

## Withholding VAT
- Government agencies: withhold 5% VAT on payments to VAT-registered suppliers
- Private sector: creditable withholding tax on certain transactions (1-15% depending on type)
- Withholding certificates (BIR Form 2307) must be issued

## Electronic Invoicing (EIS)
- BIR Revenue Regulation 2022: Electronic Invoicing System
- Mandatory for large taxpayers and eCommerce
- Computerized Accounting System (CAS) or Electronic Invoicing/Receipting System
- Must be registered with BIR before use
- E-receipts/e-invoices must have digital signature and QR code

## Key Rules
- Monthly VAT return (BIR Form 2550M) due 20th of following month
- Quarterly VAT return (BIR Form 2550Q) due 25th of month following quarter
- Input VAT credits: must have valid VAT invoice/receipt
- Excess input VAT: carried forward (refund only for zero-rated/exporters)
- Percentage Tax (3%): alternative for businesses ≤PHP 3M (BIR Form 2551Q)

## Common Pitfalls
- Using non-BIR registered receipts (invalid for input credit)
- Not issuing separate SI (goods) vs OR (services)
- Government withholding (5%) complicates VAT filing
- Forgetting BIR permit number on invoices
- Senior citizen/PWD 20% discount: separate line item, VAT-exempt
- Digital transactions: BIR now monitoring online platforms
