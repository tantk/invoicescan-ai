# Denmark — Moms (Mervaerdiafgift) Guide

## Overview
Denmark's VAT, known as Moms (Mervaerdiafgift), is administered by Skattestyrelsen (the Danish Tax Agency, commonly referred to as SKAT). Denmark has a standard VAT rate of 25% and is notable among EU member states for having no reduced VAT rates — one of very few EU countries with a single-rate VAT system. The system follows EU VAT Directives with Danish-specific implementations.

## Tax Rates
- **Standard rate: 25%** (the only rate — no reduced rates)
- **Zero-rated (0%)**: Exports of goods outside the EU, intra-EU supplies of goods (with valid buyer VAT ID), international transport, newspapers (printed, by subscription — zero-rated rather than reduced)
- **Exempt (no VAT, no input credit)**: Financial and insurance services, healthcare, education (public and certain private), residential property rental, postal services (universal service obligation), social services, cultural activities by public institutions, passenger transport, funeral services, sports (non-commercial)

### 2026 Changes
- **Fitness and wellness services**: From January 1, 2026, group training, yoga classes, and personal coaching services are newly VAT-liable at 25% (previously exempt) if turnover exceeds the registration threshold

## Tax ID Format
- **CVR number** (Det Centrale Virksomhedsregister / Central Business Register): 8-digit number identifying the business entity
  - Example: `12345678`
- **SE number**: Used specifically for VAT registration and reporting — often the same as the CVR for single-registration businesses
- **VAT number (Momsregistreringsnummer)**: Format `DK` + 8 digits (same as CVR number)
  - Example: `DK12345678`
- Verify at: https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/ (EU VIES) or https://datacvr.virk.dk

## Invoice Requirements
### Full VAT Invoice (momsfaktura)
1. Invoice date (fakturadato)
2. Unique sequential invoice number (fakturanummer)
3. Seller's full name and address
4. Seller's CVR number
5. Buyer's full name and address
6. Buyer's VAT number (for B2B and intra-EU transactions)
7. Date of supply (leveringsdato) if different from invoice date
8. Description of goods or services
9. Quantity and unit price (exclusive of Moms)
10. Net amount
11. Moms rate (25%)
12. Moms amount
13. Total amount inclusive of Moms
14. For exempt supplies: reference to the applicable Danish or EU VAT provision
15. For reverse charge: "Omvendt betalingspligt" notation
16. For intra-EU: both parties' VAT IDs and reference to the exemption
17. Currency (if not DKK, the exchange rate must be stated)
18. Payment terms

### Simplified Invoice (forenklet faktura, for supplies up to DKK 3,000 including Moms)
1. Seller's name and CVR number
2. Invoice date
3. Description of goods or services
4. Total amount inclusive of Moms
5. Moms rate

## E-Invoicing and Digital Requirements
- **B2G**: Mandatory since 2005 — Denmark was one of the first countries in the world to mandate e-invoicing for public sector supplies (originally via OIOUBL/NemHandel, now transitioning to Peppol BIS Billing 3.0)
- **B2B**: Not yet mandatory — electronic invoices widely used but PDF/paper still valid
- **Digital bookkeeping mandate (2026)**: Businesses with annual VAT-registered turnover exceeding DKK 300,000 must use a certified digital accounting system capable of sending/receiving e-invoices and generating SAF-T files for automated tax audits
- **NemHandel**: Denmark's national e-document exchange network — being aligned with Peppol
- **Archiving**: Invoices must be stored for **5 years** after the end of the financial year

## Key Rules
- **Registration threshold**: DKK 50,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic businesses. Non-resident businesses must register from the first taxable supply in Denmark (no threshold).
- **Filing frequency**: Depends on annual turnover:
  - Over DKK 50 million: monthly (due by the 25th of the following month)
  - DKK 5 million – 50 million: quarterly
  - Under DKK 5 million: semi-annually (half-yearly)
- **Tax point**: Generally the earlier of goods delivery/service completion or invoice issuance
- **Reverse charge (omvendt betalingspligt)**: Applies to services from non-resident suppliers, certain construction services, and sales of mobile phones/IT hardware above DKK 5,000 per delivery
- **One-Stop Shop (OSS)**: Denmark participates in the EU OSS scheme for B2C cross-border digital services and distance sales
- **Intrastat**: Reporting required for intra-EU goods trade above thresholds
- **EC Sales List**: Required for intra-EU supplies of goods and services

## Common Pitfalls
1. **No reduced rates exist**: Unlike most EU countries, Denmark taxes everything at 25% — do not apply a reduced rate to food, books, or transport
2. **Fitness/wellness 2026 change**: New VAT liability on group training, yoga, and coaching from January 1, 2026 — previously exempt services
3. **B2G e-invoicing**: Sending PDF or paper invoices to Danish public sector entities will be rejected — structured electronic invoices are mandatory
4. **Digital bookkeeping requirement**: Businesses over DKK 300,000 turnover must use certified accounting software with SAF-T capability from 2026
5. **Exempt vs. outside scope**: Passenger transport is exempt (no input recovery), not zero-rated — a common source of confusion
6. **Non-resident registration**: Foreign businesses making taxable supplies in Denmark must register from the first transaction — no minimum threshold
7. **CVR vs. SE confusion**: While often the same number, they serve different functions — the CVR is the business entity number, the SE number is the VAT/tax reporting number
8. **Semi-annual filers**: Small businesses filing half-yearly may miss changes in their reporting obligations when turnover grows
