# Ghana — VAT + NHIL + GETFund Guide

## Overview

Ghana levies three consumption-based taxes that are collectively applied to the supply of goods and services: **VAT** (Value Added Tax), **NHIL** (National Health Insurance Levy), and **GETFund** (Ghana Education Trust Fund Levy). These are administered by the **GRA** (Ghana Revenue Authority). Effective January 1, 2026, under the new Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151), the tax structure was overhauled — the old cascading system was eliminated, and all three levies are now charged on the same base value, giving a combined effective rate of **20%**. The previous 1% COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy has been abolished. Compliance is enforced through Ghana's E-VAT system, which operates as a real-time clearance model.

## Tax Rates

- **VAT: 15%** — standard rate on most taxable goods and services
- **NHIL: 2.5%** — National Health Insurance Levy
- **GETFund: 2.5%** — Ghana Education Trust Fund Levy
- **Combined rate: 20%** — all three levied on the same base price (effective January 1, 2026)
- **Flat rate: 4%** — available for qualifying retailers and wholesalers with annual turnover between GHS 200,000 and GHS 500,000 (replaces the old 3% flat rate)
- **Zero-rated (0%)**:
  - Exports of goods
  - Exports of services consumed outside Ghana
  - Locally manufactured textiles (extended to 2028)
  - Supplies to Free Zone enterprises
  - International transport services
- **Exempt**:
  - Unprocessed foodstuffs (fresh vegetables, fruits, grains, tubers)
  - Medical and pharmaceutical services
  - Essential drugs produced or supplied in Ghana (as listed under HS Code Chapter 30)
  - Active ingredients for essential drugs
  - Selected imported special drugs (approved by Minister for Health and Parliament)
  - Education services
  - Financial services (banking, insurance)
  - Residential property (granting, assignment, or surrender of interest in land/buildings for dwelling)
  - Right to occupy land or buildings (excluding hotel, warehousing, and storage)
  - Water supply and sewerage services
  - Postal services
  - Public transport

**Note:** Under Act 1151, betting, gaming, and lottery activities are no longer exempt. Management fees for private equity, venture capital, and mutual funds have also lost their exempt status.

## Tax ID Format

**TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)**

- **Format:** For individual taxpayers, the Ghana Card Personal Identification Number (Ghana Card PIN) serves as the TIN since April 1, 2021. For businesses and legal entities, GRA issues a separate TIN.
- **Structure:** The TIN is alphanumeric and is used as the VAT number upon VAT registration.
- **Requirement:** All businesses making taxable supplies must register for VAT and obtain a TIN from GRA.
- **Example:** `C0012345678` (corporate) or Ghana Card PIN for individuals
- **Verification:** [GRA TIN Verification Portal](https://gra.gov.gh/tin/)

## Invoice Requirements

Under the E-VAT system, every invoice must contain:

1. Seller's company/business name
2. Seller's TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)
3. Seller's location and full address
4. Buyer's name
5. Buyer's TIN (for B2B transactions)
6. Buyer's address
7. Unique invoice number (system-generated)
8. Date and time of issue
9. Description of goods or services
10. Quantity and unit of measure
11. Unit price (excluding taxes)
12. VAT amount (15% of base)
13. NHIL amount (2.5% of base)
14. GETFund amount (2.5% of base)
15. Total tax amount
16. Total amount payable
17. GRA-issued SDC (Sales Data Controller) code
18. QR code (GRA-generated for authentication)
19. Digital stamp (signature generated by the certified system as proof of authenticity)

## E-Invoicing

- **Status:** Mandatory through the E-VAT system administered by GRA.
- **Model:** Clearance model — every invoice must be transmitted to GRA's Virtual Sales Data Controller (VSDC) for approval before it can be issued to the customer.
- **Real-time clearance:** For online systems, clearance happens in real time. GRA validates each invoice and returns an SDC code with a timestamp, invoice ID, hash, and QR code.
- **Offline capability:** For offline systems, invoices can be stamped locally using certified fiscal devices, but must be transmitted to GRA within 24 hours once connectivity is restored.
- **Certified systems:** Businesses must use GRA-certified invoicing software or fiscal devices that integrate with the VSDC.
- **E-VAT platform:** GRA's digital platform for managing all VAT transactions, compliance monitoring, and reporting.
- **Monthly filing:** VAT returns must be filed by the last working day of the month following the taxable period.

## Key Rules

- **Act 1151 overhaul (January 2026):** The new VAT Act repeals and replaces the old Act 870. Businesses must ensure compliance with the new structure — all three levies (VAT, NHIL, GETFund) are now on the same base, not cascading.
- **No more cascading:** Under the old system, NHIL and GETFund were added to the cost base before applying VAT. The new system charges all levies on the same base price, reducing the effective cost to consumers.
- **Flat rate scheme changes:** The flat rate increased from 3% to 4% and is available for retailers/wholesalers with annual turnover between GHS 200,000 and GHS 500,000.
- **Newly taxable activities:** Betting, gaming, lottery, and fund management fees are now taxable under Act 1151.
- **Input tax credit:** Available only against E-VAT-validated invoices bearing a valid SDC code and QR code. Invoices without GRA clearance cannot support input credit claims.
- **Reverse charge on imported services:** Registered persons receiving services from non-resident suppliers must self-assess VAT, NHIL, and GETFund.
- **Withholding VAT:** Designated agents (government bodies, large entities) must withhold 7% of the invoice value as VAT at source and remit to GRA.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Still using the old cascading calculation method** — Under Act 1151, all three levies are on the same base. Using the old method (adding NHIL and GETFund to the base before applying VAT) results in overcharging and non-compliance.
2. **Issuing invoices without E-VAT clearance** — Ghana operates a clearance model. Invoices not transmitted to GRA's VSDC and lacking the SDC code and QR code are legally invalid.
3. **Not updating systems for the new combined 20% rate** — The shift from the old multi-layered structure (which yielded approximately 21.9%) to the clean 20% requires system reconfiguration.
4. **Overlooking newly taxable activities** — Betting, gaming, lottery, and fund management services that were previously exempt are now taxable. Businesses in these sectors must register and charge accordingly.
5. **Failing to transmit offline invoices within 24 hours** — When connectivity is restored, locally stamped invoices must be transmitted to GRA within 24 hours. Missing this window creates compliance issues.
6. **Not obtaining GRA-certified invoicing software** — Only certified systems can connect to the VSDC. Using non-certified software means invoices cannot be cleared by GRA.
7. **Missing the flat rate scheme eligibility criteria** — The scheme is only available for retailers/wholesalers within the GHS 200,000-500,000 turnover band. Businesses outside this range must use the standard rate.
8. **Ignoring reverse charge on imported services** — Failing to self-assess VAT, NHIL, and GETFund on services received from non-residents creates a hidden tax liability.
