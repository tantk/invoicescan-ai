# Sri Lanka — VAT (Value Added Tax) Guide

## Overview

Sri Lanka's Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax on the supply of goods and services. It is administered by the **IRD** (Inland Revenue Department / Department of Inland Revenue). The current standard rate is 18%, effective since January 1, 2024. As of July 2025, all VAT returns must be filed electronically. A new standardized tax invoice format became mandatory on January 1, 2026 (per Gazette Extraordinary No. 2463/05). Sri Lanka is also implementing VAT on cross-border digital services from April 1, 2026.

## Tax Rates

- **Standard rate: 18%** — applies to most goods and services
- **Zero-rated (0%)**:
  - Exports of goods
  - Exports of services (services consumed outside Sri Lanka)
  - Supplies to BOI-approved enterprises (Board of Investment projects under specific agreements)
  - International transport services
- **Exempt**:
  - Financial services (banking, insurance, stock exchange transactions)
  - Health and medical services
  - Education services
  - Public transport
  - Unprocessed agricultural products
  - Supply of water by government authorities
  - Residential property leases
  - Postal services provided by Sri Lanka Post
  - Supply of goods and services by charitable institutions
  - Certain essential food items (rice, bread, wheat flour, milk, dried fish)

## Tax ID Format

**TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)**

- **Format:** Numeric code issued by the Department of Inland Revenue upon registration
- **Structure:** TIN is linked to the individual or entity's tax profile. VAT-registered persons receive a separate VAT Registration Number linked to their TIN.
- **Requirement:** As of 2026, all persons and entities engaged in taxable economic activity must register with IRD and obtain a TIN. The VAT registration threshold is being reduced from LKR 60 million to LKR 36 million annual turnover, effective April 1, 2026.
- **Verification:** [IRD e-Services Portal](https://www.ird.gov.lk/en/eServices/)

## Invoice Requirements

Effective January 1, 2026, the new standardized format (per Gazette No. 2463/05) requires:

1. Document must be prominently titled **"Tax Invoice"** in bold or highlighted text
2. Supplier's TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)
3. Supplier's name (legal/business name)
4. Supplier's address
5. Supplier's telephone number
6. Purchaser's TIN
7. Purchaser's name
8. Purchaser's address
9. Purchaser's telephone number
10. Invoice serial number in the format **YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXX** (where YY = last 2 digits of year, MMM = first 3 letters of month in English capitals; total length max 40 characters, no spaces)
11. Date of invoice (in MM/DD/YYYY format)
12. Date of delivery (in MM/DD/YYYY format)
13. Description of goods or services
14. Quantity and unit of measure
15. Unit price
16. Net amount (exclusive of VAT)
17. VAT rate (18%)
18. VAT amount charged
19. Total consideration (inclusive of VAT)
20. Mode of payment (Cash, Cheque, Bank Transfer, Online Payment, etc.)
21. All amounts in Sri Lankan Rupees (LKR), stated without cents

Note: Supplier details must appear in the top-left corner and purchaser details in the top-right corner of the invoice.

## E-Invoicing

- **Status:** No mandatory centralized e-invoicing platform yet (as of early 2026), but all VAT returns must be filed electronically via the IRD portal since July 1, 2025.
- **Standardized format:** The new mandatory tax invoice format (effective January 1, 2026) imposes strict formatting requirements that facilitate future digital processing.
- **Digital services VAT:** From April 1, 2026, non-resident providers of digital services (streaming, apps, SaaS, online gaming, e-learning) must charge, collect, and remit 18% VAT on sales to Sri Lankan consumers.
- **Filing:** Monthly VAT returns filed electronically via the IRD portal.
- **Future direction:** Sri Lanka is expected to move toward a full e-invoicing mandate in coming years, building on the standardized invoice format and electronic filing infrastructure.

## Key Rules

- **Threshold reduction:** From April 1, 2026, the VAT registration threshold drops from LKR 60 million to LKR 36 million annual turnover. Businesses approaching this threshold must monitor and register proactively.
- **New invoice format is strict:** The January 2026 standardized format has precise requirements for field placement, date format (MM/DD/YYYY), serial number format (YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXX), and currency display (LKR, no cents). Non-compliant invoices are invalid.
- **Input tax credit:** Available only against valid tax invoices containing the supplier's TIN and all mandatory fields. Credits must be claimed in the period the invoice is received.
- **Monthly filing:** VAT returns must be filed and tax paid by the 20th of the month following the taxable period.
- **Reverse charge on imported services:** Registered persons receiving services from non-residents must self-assess and pay VAT under the reverse charge mechanism.
- **Withholding VAT:** Certain government agencies and designated entities are required to withhold VAT from payments to suppliers.
- **Capital goods:** VAT paid on capital goods used for taxable supplies is fully creditable in the period of acquisition.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Not adopting the new invoice format by January 1, 2026** — The standardized tax invoice format is mandatory. Invoices not conforming to Gazette No. 2463/05 specifications are invalid and cannot support input tax credits for the buyer.
2. **Using incorrect date format** — Sri Lanka's new invoice format requires MM/DD/YYYY. Using DD/MM/YYYY (common in the region) renders the invoice non-compliant.
3. **Including cents in invoice amounts** — All amounts must be in whole LKR, without cents. This is an unusual requirement that many businesses overlook.
4. **Incorrect invoice serial number format** — The serial number must follow the YYMMM_QQQQ_XXXX pattern with a maximum of 40 characters and no spaces. Deviating from this format invalidates the invoice.
5. **Missing the new LKR 36 million threshold** — Businesses that were below the old LKR 60 million threshold may now be required to register from April 2026. Failure to register on time triggers penalties.
6. **Not filing returns electronically** — Since July 2025, paper returns are no longer accepted. All filings must go through the IRD e-portal.
7. **Failing to apply reverse charge on imported services** — Services received from non-residents are subject to VAT under reverse charge. Not self-assessing creates a tax liability with interest and penalties.
8. **Not specifying mode of payment on invoices** — The new format requires explicit mention of the payment method. This is a new field many businesses forget to include.
